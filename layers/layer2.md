## 1. 时态完备

"present perfect continuous" is a noun.
The meaning of "present perfect continuous" is "the tense for actions that started before now and are going on now; we make it with have or has, been, and the present participle".
Example: "I have been eating."

"past perfect" is a noun.
The meaning of "past perfect" is "the tense for things that happened before a past time; we make it with had and the past participle".
Example: "I had eaten before he came."

"past perfect continuous" is a noun.
The meaning of "past perfect continuous" is "the tense for actions that were going on before a past time; we make it with had been and the present participle".
Example: "I had been eating before he came."

"future continuous" is a noun.
The meaning of "future continuous" is "the tense for actions that will be going on at a future time; we make it with will be and the present participle".
Example: "I will be eating at night."

"future perfect" is a noun.
The meaning of "future perfect" is "the tense for things that will be done before a future time; we make it with will have and the past participle".
Example: "I will have eaten before night."

"already" is an adverb.
The meaning of "already" is "before now; at a time before this time".
Example: "I have already eaten."

"ever" is an adverb.
The meaning of "ever" is "at any time".
Example: "Have you ever seen the sun?"

"just" is an adverb.
The meaning of "just" is "a very short time before now".
Example: "I have just eaten."

"morning" is a noun.
The meaning of "morning" is "the start of the day".
Example: "I work in the morning."

"since" is a preposition.
The meaning of "since" is "from a past time to now".
Example: "I have been here since morning."

"finish" is a verb.
The meaning of "finish" is "stop doing something; do all of something".
Example: "I finish my work at night."

"result" is a noun.
The meaning of "result" is "something that happens because of other things".
Example: "The result of work is money."

"soon" is an adverb.
The meaning of "soon" is "after a short time".
Example: "We will eat soon."

We use the past simple for a finished time: I ate yesterday.
We use the present perfect for a time that is not finished, or when the result is true now: I have eaten food, so I do not need more food.
We use 'already' in positive sentences: I have already eaten.
We use 'ever' in questions: Have you ever seen the sun?
We use 'just' for a very short time before now: I have just eaten.
We use 'since' with a past time: I have been here since morning.
We use 'for' with a time: I have been here for two days.

## 2. 情态动词

"able" is an adjective.
The meaning of "able" is "can do something".
Example: "I am able to see the sun."

"allow" is a verb.
The meaning of "allow" is "say that someone can do something; say yes to something".
Example: "They allow me to eat."

"permission" is a noun.
The meaning of "permission" is "when someone says that you can do something".
Example: "He gives me permission to eat."

"polite" is an adjective.
The meaning of "polite" is "of a person: good to other people".
Example: "He is a polite man."

"have to" is a verb.
The meaning of "have to" is "must; we use it when something must happen or be true".
Example: "I have to eat."

"used to" is a verb.
The meaning of "used to" is "a way to say that something happened many times in the past but does not happen now".
Example: "I used to live here."

"would rather" is a verb.
The meaning of "would rather" is "we use it to say that someone wants one thing more than other things".
Example: "I would rather eat than sleep."

We use 'can' to say that someone is able to do something: I can see the sun.
We use 'can' or 'may' to ask for permission: Can I eat? May I go?
We use 'may', 'might', or 'could' for things that may happen: He might come tomorrow.
We use 'must' for things that must happen or be true: You must eat food.
We use 'must not' to say that something is not allowed: You must not go.
We use 'do not have to' to say that something is not needed: You do not have to come.
We use 'should' for what is good or right to do: You should eat food every day.
We use 'would' for polite questions: Would you like some water?
We use 'used to' for things that happened many times in the past but do not happen now: I used to live here.

## 3. 被动语态

"active" is an adjective.
The meaning of "active" is "of a sentence: the person or thing that does something is the subject".
Example: "In 'He eats food', the sentence is active."

"passive" is an adjective.
The meaning of "passive" is "of a sentence: the person or thing that something is done to is the subject".
Example: "In 'The food is eaten by the man', the sentence is passive."

We make the passive with be and the past participle: The food is eaten by the man.
We use the passive when we do not know or do not want to say who does something: The food was eaten.
We use 'by' to say who does something in the passive: The food is eaten by the man.

## 4. 比较

"compare" is a verb.
The meaning of "compare" is "look at two or more things and see how they are the same or different".
Example: "We compare the sun and the moon."

"comparative" is a noun.
The meaning of "comparative" is "the form of an adjective or adverb that we use with 'than' to say that one thing has more of something than other things".
Example: "Older is the comparative of old."

"superlative" is a noun.
The meaning of "superlative" is "the form of an adjective or adverb that says that one thing has the most of something in a group".
Example: "Biggest is the superlative of big."

We use 'than' after the comparative: He is older than me.
We make the comparative of short adjectives with 'er': older, bigger.
We make the comparative of long adjectives with 'more': more different.
We make the superlative with 'est' or 'most': the biggest, the most different.
We use 'the' before the superlative.
Some forms are different: good → better → best; bad → worse → worst.
We use 'as ... as' to say that two things are the same: He is as old as me.
We use 'not as ... as' when they are not the same: He is not as old as me.

## 5. 从句

"relative" is an adjective.
The meaning of "relative" is "of a word like who, which, that, where, or when: it starts a clause that says more about a noun".
Example: "'Who' is a relative word."

"relative clause" is a noun.
The meaning of "relative clause" is "a clause that starts with a relative word".
Example: "In 'the man who sees me', 'who sees me' is a relative clause."

"whether" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "whether" is "a word like 'if'; we use it for questions that need yes or no".
Example: "I do not know whether he is here."

"unless" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "unless" is "if not".
Example: "I will not go unless he comes."

"although" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "although" is "a word like 'but'; we use it at the start of a clause".
Example: "Although he is old, he works."

"contrast" is a noun.
The meaning of "contrast" is "when two things are not the same".
Example: "The contrast between day and night is big."

"even though" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "even though" is "although; we use it for a bigger contrast".
Example: "Even though he is old, he works."

"reason" is a noun.
The meaning of "reason" is "the word or words that say why something happens or is true".
Example: "The reason is that he is old."

"purpose" is a noun.
The meaning of "purpose" is "the reason for doing something".
Example: "The purpose of eating is to live."

"so that" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "so that" is "we use it to say the purpose of doing something".
Example: "I eat so that I can work."

"as if" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "as if" is "like; we use it when something is not true but looks or feels true".
Example: "He looks as if he is old."

"while" is a conjunction.
The meaning of "while" is "at the same time as something happens".
Example: "He sleeps while I work."

We use 'who' for people, 'which' for things, 'where' for places, 'when' for times, and 'whose' for what is of a person or thing.
We can use 'that' for people or things.
We can use a relative clause with no relative word when the relative word is the object: the man I see.
We use 'when', 'before', 'after', and 'while' for time: He comes when I work; I eat before I sleep.
We use 'because' and 'since' for reasons: He works because he needs money; I sleep since it is night.
We use 'so that' for purposes: I work so that I can eat.
We use 'although' and 'even though' for contrast: Although he is old, he works; even though he is old, he works.
We use 'so ... that' for results: He is so old that he does not work.

## 6. 条件句

"condition" is a noun.
The meaning of "condition" is "something that must be true or happen for other things to be true or happen".
Example: "'If he comes' is a condition."

"conditional" is an adjective.
The meaning of "conditional" is "of a sentence: it has 'if' and says what happens when something happens".
Example: "'If he comes, I will see him' is a conditional sentence."

"wish" is a verb.
The meaning of "wish" is "want something that is not true now or may not happen".
Example: "I wish I had money."

The zero conditional is if + present simple + present simple: If he works, he gets money.
The first conditional is if + present simple + will + verb: If he comes, I will see him.
The second conditional is if + past simple + would + verb: If I had money, I would get food.
The third conditional is if + past perfect + would have + past participle: If I had seen him, I would have told him.
We use 'unless' in conditionals; unless means 'if not': I will not go unless he comes.
We use 'if' and 'unless' for conditions: I go if he comes; I do not go unless he comes.
We use 'wish' with the past simple for things that are not true now: I wish I had money.
We use 'wish' with the past perfect for past things that we want to be different: I wish I had seen him.

## 7. 间接引语

"change" is a verb.
The meaning of "change" is "make something different; start to be different".
Example: "The day changes to night."

"report" is a verb.
The meaning of "report" is "say what someone said".
Example: "I report his words."

"direct speech" is a noun.
The meaning of "direct speech" is "the same words that someone said".
Example: "'I am old' is direct speech."

"reported speech" is a noun.
The meaning of "reported speech" is "the way we say what someone said; we often change the tense and the pronouns".
Example: "'He said that he was old' is reported speech."

"next" is an adjective.
The meaning of "next" is "the one that comes after this one".
Example: "The next day is tomorrow."

We use 'say' with 'that': He says that he is old.
We use 'tell' with a person: He tells me that he is old.
In reported speech, we change the present to the past: He says → he said; I am → I was.
We change today to that day, here to there, this to that, and tomorrow to the next day.
We report a question with 'if' or 'whether': He asks me if I am old.
We report a question word with the same question word: He asks me where I live.

## 8. 非谓语

"infinitive" is a noun.
The meaning of "infinitive" is "the form of a verb with 'to' before it, like 'to eat'".
Example: "To eat is an infinitive."

"gerund" is a noun.
The meaning of "gerund" is "the form of a verb with 'ing' that we use like a noun, like 'eating'".
Example: "Eating is a gerund."

We use the infinitive after 'want', 'need', and 'would like': I want to eat; I need to see the sun; I would like to work.
We use the gerund after 'like': I like eating.
We can use the gerund or the infinitive after 'like': I like eating; I like to eat.

## 9. 疑问补充

"tag question" is a noun.
The meaning of "tag question" is "a short question that we put after a sentence".
Example: "'Is he not?' is a tag question."

"neither" is a determiner.
The meaning of "neither" is "not this one and not that one; not any one of two".
Example: "Neither man is old."

A positive sentence has a negative tag question: He is old, is he not?
A negative sentence has a positive tag question: He is not old, is he?
We use 'so do I' to say that the same is true for me: He works, and so do I.
We use 'neither do I' for the negative: He does not work, and neither do I.

> 说明：isn't / aren't / don't / can't 这类短形式 = is not / are not / do not / cannot。
> 本项目里的定义和例句一律写完整形式；日常口语里的短形式以后自然就会。

## 10. there be 与 it

We use 'there is' and 'there are' to say that something is in a place: There is water in the earth; there are many people here.
We use 'it' for the time and the day: It is night; it is a new day.
We use 'it' for a thing that we say after the verb: It is good to work.

## 11. 连接词

"however" is an adverb.
The meaning of "however" is "a word like 'but'; we use it at the start of a new sentence".
Example: "He is old. However, he works."

"therefore" is an adverb.
The meaning of "therefore" is "a word like 'so'; we use it at the start of a new sentence".
Example: "He needs money. Therefore, he works."

"last" is an adjective.
The meaning of "last" is "after all other ones".
Example: "Night is the last part of the day."

"finally" is an adverb.
The meaning of "finally" is "after all the other things".
Example: "Finally, I eat."

"then" is an adverb.
The meaning of "then" is "after that; at that time".
Example: "I eat now, and then I sleep."

We use 'however' at the start of a new sentence for contrast: He is old. However, he works.
We use 'therefore' at the start of a new sentence for a result: He needs money. Therefore, he works.
We use 'first', 'then', and 'finally' for the way that one thing comes after other things: First I eat; then I work; finally I sleep.
